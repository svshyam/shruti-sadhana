Any raga can be edited, including the built-in ones, to match your patantharam (e.g. Behag, Begada, Kedaragowla). Click Edit selected raga, change the arohana/avarohana, then Save changes. Edited ragas show ✎ in the list, and Restore original brings back the built-in version.

How it works

The microphone signal is analysed about 60 times a second with the McLeod pitch method. Pick the student's shruti (C = 1 kattai … B = 7 kattai) and the octave of their madhya Sa; use the Hz box to fine-tune to the exact tanpura pitch. Each pitch is mapped to the nearest swara of the selected raga in any sthayi. The practice range decides which arohana/avarohana cycle is scored: mandra (.Sa to Sa), madhya (Sa to Sa'), tara (Sa' to Sa'') or all three sthayis in one continuous cycle (.Sa up to Sa'' and back). A note is counted once it is held for at least a quarter second; its pitch is the median of the steady part, ignoring the attack. Match % falls linearly from 100% when exactly on the swara to 0% at the cents value set above (default 50¢, a quarter tone). A cycle starts when you sing the first swara (Sa) and ends only after you have reached the top swara and come back down to the final Sa (or when you press Finish cycle). The whole performance is then lined up against the expected arohana and avarohana: stray notes from glides or breaths are ignored as extras, a skipped swara scores 0%, and a wrong swara is scored against the swara that was expected. The cycle score is the average over every swara.

Use headphones if you turn on the sruti drone or play reference tones, otherwise the microphone hears the drone as well as the voice.
